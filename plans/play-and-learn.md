# Playing and learning: what the game still wants

State: noted 2026-11-09, none specified. Chris asked for a look through the game, before the options desk, for
what would make it teach better and play better; it was toured headless at 1280 wide and the stages, lessons and
experiments read. Twelve points came back and Chris agreed them all. The first three (the way in) are
`plans/way-in.md`. These are the other nine and two smaller things, each with what was seen and a first idea. Each
needs its own plan before it is built.

## As a game

- **Stakes and a goal.** There is no bankruptcy, no running cost and no target. The firm starts with $200,000 and
  the first office is $8,000, so the first purchase is no decision. First ideas: a monthly rent on each rack and
  line, so a seat has to pay for itself; a target for each stage ("earn back the seat"); an end, either way (beat
  Harbor Quant over a week, or go bust).
- **Rivals as characters.** The Rivals tab is a grid of latencies. The order log already names who beat you; the
  day report could say what each rival took ("Harbor Quant took 14 quotes off you today, worth $310").
- **Pacing.** At 1× nothing happens for minutes; at 300× a day is five to ten minutes. First idea: slow of its own
  accord for a headline, a fix or an expiry, and run fast between, so the player is in front of the moments that
  teach.
- **Moments marked.** A won race, a closed triangle, a delivery, a margin call and a fine all arrive as a row in a
  list. A brief mark for the few that matter.

## For learning

- **An Overview that grows.** On the first day five of its six sections say "Nothing yet". Show the position and
  today's chart until the first close, and add each section when it has something to say.
- **Lessons that take you there.** "Open the Shop", when the Shop is half of the Strategy page. Each lesson could
  carry a link to the place, as the tutorial's cards do.
- **Each block's record, and why it loses.** `curve` and `crossPairs` lose from every seat, momentum is near
  nothing from home, the fix is no cheaper than slices. Only the experiments teach that a textbook signal can be
  worth less than the spread. A block's card could show its record so far and the reason in a line.
- **"Why did I lose money today?"** The day report splits by block, stock and race. A sentence on the largest
  single cause (fees, races lost, one headline, storage) is what a learner would read first.
- **The Market page by desk.** With four desks the watchlist is one table of 29 rows and the races table lists
  venues with nothing on them. Group by desk; hide a venue with no orders.

## Smaller

- The currency balances show "EUR 0.00" on the Overview before the currencies desk has been used.
- The shop lists every venue's latency for every rack. A line of what changes for you (your milliseconds now,
  and after, to the venues you trade) would replace most of it.
